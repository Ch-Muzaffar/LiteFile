FAQ

Common questions

What can I encode in a QR code?

Anything: URLs, plain text, email addresses, phone numbers, Wi-Fi credentials, or contact cards (vCard). Most smartphone cameras detect the content type automatically and take the right action — open a browser, dial a number, add a contact, and so on.

What is error correction level?

Error correction allows a QR code to be read even when part of it is damaged, dirty, or obscured. L recovers ~7% data loss, M ~15%, Q ~25%, H ~30%. Use H if you plan to place a logo or image inside the QR code. Use L or M for clean digital displays.

Why does my QR code fail to scan?

The most common causes: low contrast between foreground and background colors, text that is too long (making the QR code too dense), or the image is printed too small (minimum 2 cm × 2 cm recommended). Try increasing the error correction level or shortening the content with a URL shortener.

Is there a character limit?

QR codes can encode up to ~2,953 characters in byte mode (at error correction L). For typical URLs this is rarely an issue. Very long text or data strings may exceed the limit — the generator will warn you when you're approaching it.

PNG or SVG — which should I use?

Use PNG for digital use: websites, emails, presentations, and screen display. Use SVG for print: it scales to any size without pixelation, making it ideal for business cards, posters, and product packaging.
