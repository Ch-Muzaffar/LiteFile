Questions

Frequently asked questions

Why is my compressed PDF larger than the original?

This tool works by rasterizing every page into a JPEG image and rebuilding the PDF from those images. It's effective for image-heavy and scanned files, but counterproductive for text-based documents.

A PDF exported from Word or Google Docs stores text and fonts as compact vector data. Converting that to JPEG adds overhead — a 200 KB text document can easily become 800 KB. If this happens, LiteFile will warn you and offer the original file unchanged.

How much can I reduce a PDF file size?

It depends entirely on the content. Scanned documents and PDFs with large embedded photos can shrink by 60–80%. Mixed documents (text with images) typically see 20–50% reductions at the 75% quality setting.

If you need to reduce a text-heavy PDF, consider re-exporting it from the original application at a lower quality, or using Split PDF to remove pages you don't need.

Does compressing a PDF reduce visual quality?

Yes, slightly. At 75% quality (the default), the visual difference is minimal when viewing on screen. Text may appear slightly softer compared to a native vector PDF because it has been rasterized into pixels.

If the document will be printed or shared professionally, use 85–95% quality to keep text sharp. For email attachments and web uploads where file size matters more than perfect fidelity, 60–75% is typically fine.

What types of PDFs benefit most from compression?

The best candidates are:

  • Scanned documents — photos of pages saved directly as PDFs
  • PDFs exported from Adobe Illustrator, InDesign, or Photoshop
  • Product catalogues, brochures, and portfolios with photography
  • PDFs with embedded high-resolution images or uncompressed graphics

PDFs from Word, Excel, Google Docs, or code-generated output (invoices, reports) benefit the least.

Can I compress a password-protected PDF?

No. Password-protected PDFs cannot be opened or rendered by browser-based tools. Remove the password protection from the file first — most PDF readers let you do this via File → Export or Print to PDF — then compress it with LiteFile.
