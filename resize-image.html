Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does resizing an image reduce its quality?

Scaling an image down reduces its pixel count — this is irreversible, and you cannot recover detail that wasn't captured at the original resolution.

If you export as JPEG or WebP, the quality slider controls additional lossy compression. A setting of 85% gives a clean result for most photos. PNG export is always lossless, regardless of the dimensions you choose.

Will my image be uploaded to a server?

No. Resizing runs entirely in your browser using the Canvas API — a built-in browser technology. Your image is never transmitted to LiteFile or any third party.

This also means the tool works fully offline once the page has loaded.

What is the maximum image size I can resize?

LiteFile accepts images up to 50 MB. Very large images above 6000×6000 px may take a moment depending on your device's memory and CPU. There is no server-side limit.

Output dimensions are capped at 8000 px per side — sufficient for 4K UHD output and most professional use cases.

What is the difference between JPG, PNG, and WebP?

JPG uses lossy compression — excellent for photographs, produces small files, but does not support transparency.

PNG uses lossless compression — larger files but pixel-perfect results; supports transparent backgrounds, ideal for logos and screenshots.

WebP is a modern format offering better compression than both JPG and PNG at equivalent quality, with full transparency support. Supported by all current browsers.

What does "lock aspect ratio" mean?

Aspect ratio is the proportional relationship between an image's width and height — for example, 16:9 for Full HD video. With the lock on, changing the width automatically adjusts the height (and vice versa) so your image is never stretched or squished.

Turn the lock off if you need exact canvas dimensions — for instance, fitting a specific platform upload requirement where the image will be cropped or letterboxed anyway.
