Questions

Frequently asked questions

Which video formats are supported?

LiteFile supports MP4, MOV, AVI, MKV, and WebM as input formats. The tool uses FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, which supports the same wide range of codecs and containers as desktop FFmpeg — most common video files will work.

How long does conversion take?

The first time you use this tool, FFmpeg WebAssembly (~8 MB) needs to download — this takes 5–15 seconds on a typical connection. Subsequent conversions on the same page are immediate.

Actual audio extraction speed depends on the video length and your device's CPU. Most videos complete within seconds to a couple of minutes.

What bitrate should I choose?

128 kbps — standard quality, suitable for speech, podcasts, and casual listening. Files are smaller.

192 kbps — good quality for music. Most people cannot reliably hear a difference above 192 kbps. Recommended for most music.

320 kbps — maximum MP3/M4A quality, essentially indistinguishable from the original audio. Best for archival or audiophile listening, but produces larger files.

Is my video uploaded to a server?

No. LiteFile uses FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, which runs entirely inside your browser. Your video file never leaves your device — not to LiteFile's servers or any third party. This is an architectural guarantee: there is no server-side processing infrastructure.

What is the difference between MP3 and M4A?

MP3 is the most universally compatible audio format — it works on every device, player, and platform without exception.

M4A (AAC audio in an MP4 container) is more efficient, producing slightly better audio quality at the same bitrate with a slightly smaller file size. All Apple devices, Android, and modern web browsers support M4A. Choose it if you know your playback device supports it.
